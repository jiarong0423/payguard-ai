# Sources for the size of the problem (retrieved 2026-11-09)

TradeGlot has no user study and does not claim to reduce anyone's losses or win more disputes. These sources only say how large the setting is and what PayPal asks sellers to provide. Each figure below was read on the cited page.

| Figure | Source | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 439 million active accounts at 31 December 2025 | PayPal Holdings, Form 10-K for fiscal 2025, [sec.gov](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1633917/000163391726000024/pypl-20251231.htm) | Accounts include buyers; this is not a count of sellers |
| About $1.79 trillion total payment volume in 2025 (1,793,979 million) | PayPal fourth-quarter 2025 earnings release, 3 February 2026, [8-K exhibit](https://www.boardroomalpha.com/sec/pypl-8-k-2026-02-03-0001633917-26-000021/pypl4q-25earningsrelease.htm) | Mirror of the SEC filing |
| PayPal defines nine dispute reasons, each with its own evidence types; for "item not received" the evidence types are proof of fulfilment (carrier and tracking number, or proof-of-delivery documents) and proof of refund | PayPal Developer, [Dispute reasons and evidence](https://developer.paypal.com/disputes/reasons-evidence) | This is the evidence TradeGlot helps a seller assemble |

**Not used, on purpose.** Fee amounts and response deadlines (sources disagree and the PayPal help pages could not be read), the share of disputes that are "item not received" (no sourced figure found), seller-side loss rates, and vendor surveys of friendly fraud (self-reported, with a commercial interest). Loss totals in PayPal's filing combine transaction and credit losses, so they are not a measure of seller disputes.
